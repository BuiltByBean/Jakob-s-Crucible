### I am Darth Vader

### INTRO

Greetings, and **welcome** to —
**The Wisdom Crucible** with Jakob McClain —
where you and I engage in the **ongoing** practice
of applying **biblical** thinking
to deepen our **understanding** of biblical **truth**.

This is a short reflection —
meant to remind us that God can speak through stories,
and reveal truth in unexpected ways.

That said, consider the following
an allegorical reflection.

### ALLEGORICAL REFLECTION

I never intended to become a villain.

*(sigh)*

In the beginning,
my dream was to become a hero —
a knight —
who’s noble deeds would match
or exceed those of my story-time heroes.

I latched onto the notion
that gaining enough power —
strength, knowledge, and resources —
would enable me to save myself
and my loved ones from suffering.

I aspired to rise above the dust I was born in —
to soar on the wings of my own imagined trajectory
fueled by the force of expectations
from within and without.

Even then,
even in what seemed the innocence of youth,
there was the **duplicity** of selfish desire
hiding in the shadow of virtuous aspiration.

I wanted to serve and protect,
to secure and provide.

Even still, I **desired** the **power**.
I desired a reputation for stature
and admiration for accolades,
which was never more clear
than in the frustrating moments of feeling held back
by forces outside of my control.

My imagination depicted grand scenes of the future,
more evanescent whims than concrete visions,
yet still, they seemed tangible enough to my young mind.

With unquestionable certainty I reached for the stars,
not yet having grasped the measure of my limitations.

My journey soon landed me in the true heart of civilization —
towering testaments of technological and philosophical advancements
hiding long and deep shadows with their artificial lights.

Slowly at first, and then all at once,
I realized that there was no level of governance,
no circle of status,
free from the corruption of imperfect people.

I could see that there were systems in place
designed to enable people to debate
and decide the best course of action and then do it,
but I could also see that it wasn’t working,
not well enough for me —
not well enough to silence the cries
of the oppressed and suffering.

In my ignorant frustration,
and my veiled ambition,
I sought to enforce peace, justice, and freedom
as though my circumstances were my empire to rule.

I **reached** for **control** when I should have **given** **trust**.

I may have denied such intent,
but I acted as though to exert my will
over the guiding force of the universe,
rather than to submit and trust in my omnipresent ally —
as though my hands could contain such a thing.

Power is a fine tool:
beautiful when first beheld,
with an enticing radiance to the edge of its blade,
it beckons a touch at the expense of your hand.

Swords have hilts for a reason:
the cutting edge does not discriminate.

Only an indestructible force can grasp power at the source.
I, as many others before me,
had to make an example of myself for this lesson.

I couldn’t just listen to the wisdom of my elders —
those who were older and especially wiser,
those whom I often resisted.

I saw their restraint and discipline as a tether,
holding me back from fulfilling my self-assigned destiny —
from defeating the dark dreams of dread
and achieving salvation through **MY** works.

Before long, I had drifted from the light of hope
into the dark certainty
that **my** independent brilliance
was inevitably steering me straight into.

I was in control.
I was piloting the ship,
and that’s **exactly** what was wrong.

I feared unknown outcomes
because I didn’t trust in the force
that prophesied of future harmony.
I trusted **my** own vision over **proven** wisdom.

That fear led to frustration,
a frustration that was more than it seemed.

It was a thinly veiled anger for my lack of control —
the inability to force the outcome that I desired.

As you may know,
anger leads to hate,
and hate leads to suffering.

My anger over the **intended** design of my limitations
led to **hatred** for my condition —
a condition that I didn’t understand
and couldn’t appreciate.

That hatred for everything about myself
that I couldn’t **control**, that I couldn’t **fix**,
provided no comfort or means of improvement.

The only thing that it gave me
was **suffering**.

It gave me the **unlimited** power
to **agonize** over the **mutilation** of my **potential**,
to **regret** the **failure** of my attempt
to restrain the power of death over life.

Eventually, I looked at my reflection,
and called myself by the name of what I had become.

I am Darth Vader.

*(sigh)*

I may have started as an innocent child,
but I allowed my dark inclinations to lead me
out of innocence, to destructive consequence.

Through my weakness,
it is those who needed me the most
who have suffered the most from my wrath.

I’ve never needed conspirators
to turn others against me.
I have done that myself.

Thankfully, there was one who never gave up on me:
**The Son**, who came to my rescue
despite the deserved destiny that I had doomed myself to
through the choices that I **knowingly** and **willingly** made
to become His **enemy**.

He never gave up hope for me.
He knew that I could be good,
even though I had **destroyed** the person that He wanted me to be
through my becoming Vader.

He could have fought back and destroyed me,
but instead He fought **FOR** me
by giving Himself up to be arrested by the empire
and sentenced to death.

The sentence didn’t stick though.

Darth Vader, I was,
but no longer of the darkness, am I.
Now, I am myself.

### THE GOSPEL

I’m not speaking of **my** son,
precious though he is.

I’m speaking of **THE** Son —
The begotten **Son of God**,
who gave Himself up to **suffer** on my behalf,
that His willingness to suffer would awaken my spirit
and teach me to **love** as God loves.

He who commands the armies of Heaven **stayed** His hand,
to be **beaten**,
to be **crucified**,
to **defeat** **death** by **resurrection** after 3 days without breath.

The Son’s name is **Jesus Christ**.

In the New Testament, the Word is recorded by John to say this:
In the beginning was the Word,
and the Word was **with** God,
and the Word **was** God.

The Word became flesh and made His dwelling among us.
We have seen His glory,
the glory of the **one** and **only** Son from the Father,
full of **grace** and **truth**.

No one has ever seen God,
but the one and only Son,
who **is Himself** **God** and is at the Father’s side,
has made Him known.

For God so loved the world
that He gave His one and only Son,
that **everyone** who believes in Him **shall not perish**
but have **eternal** **life**.

If it wasn’t too late for **me**,
It’s not too late for **you**.

If you can hear this message and comprehend it,
you have the ability to offer praise to God right now
for the redemptive love of The Son
who took upon Himself the punishment that **your** **sin** **earned**.

How could you possibly choose
to throw away eternity in the perfect kingdom
that He describes in the book of Revelation.

If you can speak,
offer **genuine** words of repentance
**right now** if you haven’t already.

Even if you are mute,
if you can hear and comprehend this message
then you can offer a repentant heart
and praise in your soul.
Be assured, He **surrounds** us and **penetrates** us;
He knows our **every** **thought**.

Study the Bible.
It’s **LITERALLY** The Word of God.

Turn from the life of darkness and evil
that is **all** that your independence from Him produces in you,
and give Him your soul
to be **reborn** for the eternity that is coming.
