*The House of God and the Heart of Man | HAGGAI 1:1–2:9*

### INTRO

Greetings, and welcome to —
The Wisdom Crucible with Jakob McClain —
where you and I engage in the ongoing practice
of applying biblical thinking
to deepen our understanding of biblical truth.

Why does God sometimes let your efforts fail?

Why do seasons come when nothing seems to satisfy,
nothing seems to progress,
and all your labor slips through your fingers like sand?

God answers that question
without softening the truth —
and reveals the path back into His good graces
through Haggai.

This brief book is a message
to a people who had returned from exile
yet had not returned to the fear of the LORD;
to a people who were busy at work —
but not aligning their work with God’s will.

It begins with a prophetic confrontation
that exposes misplaced priorities,
and ends with a promise of glory
greater than any they had known.

That said, let’s dig in together.

### THE PLAN

In this episode we will examine the first 3 sections
of the book of Haggai:

1. The Command to Rebuild the Temple, chapter 1, verses 1–11
2. The People Obey the LORD, chapter 1, verses 12–15
3. The Coming Glory of the Temple, chapter 2, verses 1–9

Along the way, we’ll trace the timeline,
the repetition of names and titles,
the covenant logic,
the pattern of fear → obedience → reassurance,
and the theological weight
of God’s presence among His people.

### TIMELINE

First, I want to review the timeline of critical events
surrounding the time of Haggai:

586 BC — Jerusalem is conquered
and the first temple of Solomon is destroyed
by Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon,
and the people of Israel are exiled from their homeland.

538 BC — The Babylonian exile is ended
by King Cyrus of Persia,
and the people return to the land of Israel.

536 BC — Work on the foundation of the temple begins.

~530 BC — The work is halted
in light of certain circumstances:

grief of the older generation (mentioned in Ezra 3),

Samaritan opposition (mentioned in Ezra 4),

and political instability
immediately after King Cyrus of Persia died
during a military campaign.

His son, Cambyses II,
ruled for 7-8 tumultuous years around 530-522 BC
and died suddenly,

after which a usurper ruled for a few months,
and was deposed in 522 BC by Darius I,
who supported the remnant of Israel
in their rebuilding efforts.

520 BC — Haggai’s prophetic ministry begins.

516 BC — The second temple is completed.

445 BC — The city wall is rebuilt

~430 BC — Malachi, the final OT prophet,
is sent to prepare the way for the NT and Jesus.

### SECTION I — THE COMMAND TO REBUILD THE TEMPLE

Now for the text,
we’ll begin with the first 11 verses
of Haggai chapter 1.

*In the second year of Darius the king, in the sixth month, on the first day of the month, the word of the Lord came by the hand of Haggai the prophet to Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest: 2 “Thus says the Lord of hosts: These people say the time has not yet come to rebuild the house of the Lord.” 3 Then the word of the Lord came by the hand of Haggai the prophet, 4 “Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins? 5 Now, therefore, thus says the Lord of hosts: Consider your ways. 6 You have sown much, and harvested little. You eat, but you never have enough; you drink, but you never have your fill. You clothe yourselves, but no one is warm. And he who earns wages does so to put them into a bag with holes.*

*7 Thus says the Lord of hosts: Consider your ways. 8 Go up to the hills and bring wood and build the house, that I may take pleasure in it and that I may be glorified, says the Lord. 9 You looked for much, and behold, it came to little. And when you brought it home, I blew it away. Why? declares the Lord of hosts. Because of my house that lies in ruins, while each of you busies himself with his own house. 10 Therefore the heavens above you have withheld the dew, and the earth has withheld its produce. 11 And I have called for a drought on the land and the hills, on the grain, the new wine, the oil, on what the ground brings forth, on man and beast, and on all their labors.”*

Let’s work back through that section verse-by-verse.

V1 solidifies the timing for Haggai’s proclamation:
Day 1, Month 6, Year 2 of Darius I

Consider this:

Why is Haggai so precisely dated and addressed
compared to other prophets?

Most prophets give only general historical markers —
kings, events, or broad periods,
but Haggai gives exact day, month, and regnal year
for each oracle.

This reinforces historical reliability
by tying Haggai to Persian chronology,
and shows intentional coordination with Ezra and Zechariah
who’s ministries occurred at around the same time.

The precise and compact timeline
emphasizes the urgency of the rebuilding,
and it highlights God’s involvement in real history,
not vague spirituality.

The Word comes through Haggai to:

Zerubbabel — the political leader,
and Joshua — the high priest.

The address to Zerubbabel demonstrates clearly
that the state is not exempt
from the message of the prophet.

Politicians are not immune
to the authority of God.
God expected the political leader to care
about the state of the church.

Joshua’s address testifies
that even those who minister in God’s house
can become negligent of God’s priorities.

He needed a prophetic push
to rebuild the very house he was supposed to serve.

V2 —

*Thus says the Lord of hosts: These people say the time has not yet come to rebuild the house of the Lord.*

Their excuse is added to the indictment
that God brings against them.

Calling out their excuse highlights the insufficiency of it.

It’s like when a defendant in court
insists on explaining their motives,
and the prosecutor reminds them
that motives don’t negate crimes.

Good intentions don’t fix broken laws.

Or more relatable to parents:

It’s like when your child explains
that they didn’t have time to clean their room
because they were too busy
playing a game with their friends.

The excuse reveals misaligned priorities.

Your personal desires do NOT
come before your adherence to the law —
or your service to God.

Moving on.

V3 is repetition of V1,
introducing a pattern spanning the rest of the book.

*Then the word of the Lord came by the hand of Haggai the prophet.*

V1 contains this same declaration
of a prophetic oracle.

It re-introduces proper prophetic dialogue —
stability through consistency,
continuity through familiar phrasing,
and divine authority over human authorities
through direct formal address to Zerubbabel and Joshua.

Passages such as Deuteronomy 5:1, 6:4, and 9:1,
use this same rhetorical pattern.

*And Moses summoned all Israel and said to them, “Hear, O Israel, the statutes and the rules that I speak in your hearing today, and you shall learn them and be careful to do them.”*

*Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one. 5 You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might.*

*Hear, O Israel: you are to cross over the Jordan today, to go in to dispossess nations greater and mightier than you, cities great and fortified up to heaven.*

“Hear, O Israel”, is repeated throughout those passages
as a formal address to introduce a message from the LORD.

Back to Haggai.

V4 —

*Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?*

So, is it acceptable to neglect the house of God
while you live in finished houses with paneled walls?

Sometimes what we need to hear —
what God is saying to us —
is something that we do not want to hear.

Sometimes the truth hurts,
but it’s not because God’s a big meanie.
It’s because you are more selfish
and prone to evil than you realize.

Let me ask you this:

How wealthy is your house?
Your vacation home?

How much have you reserved for yourself
before giving anything to God?

This isn’t all about money either,
because God brought back
much of the wealth from the Persian purse,
rather than from Israel’s purses.

God’s command in V8 is not to empty their wallets,
it’s to give their time and effort,
to go up to the hills,
to retrieve the wood,
to resume the work.

You don’t have to possess ANY material wealth
to give your time and effort to God.

Let’s examine V5 —

*Now, therefore, thus says the Lord of hosts: Consider your ways.*

This is a universal principle
that is echoed throughout the scriptures.

God wants you to ponder your decisions
and their consequences —
to think carefully
about the moral direction of your life.

This is not a context-bound command,
as the instruction to the rich young ruler
in Matthew 19
to give away everything that he owned.

The proof is abundant throughout the Bible:

Proverbs 4:26 says to “ponder the path of your feet”.

Psalm 119:59 says, “when I think on my ways, I turn my feet to Your testimonies”.

Lamentations 3:40 says, “let us test and examine our ways, and return to the LORD”.

Galatians 6:4 says, “let each one test his own work”.

Ephesians 5:15 say to “look carefully then how you walk, not as unwise but as wise”.

So, applying V5 as a command to you and I
is not narrative extrapolation —
it’s canonical consensus.

Those three simple words after “thus says the LORD”
carry serious weight —
“Consider your ways.”

Let’s move on.

V6 calls out their efforts made in vain.

*You have sown much, and harvested little. You eat, but you never have enough; you drink, but you never have your fill. You clothe yourselves, but no one is warm. And he who earns wages does so to put them into a bag with holes.*

These statements reach far beyond their immediate context.

Our greed is insatiable
because it wants everything —
a right that belongs only to God.

You are designed by God and for God —
so only God can grant you fulfillment.

You might say, “I’m fulfilled by motherhood —
but I wish that I had more time to do what I want.”

You might say, “I’m fulfilled by my career —
but I wish that I had a life outside of the job.”

You won’t say, “I’m fulfilled by God in paradise —
but I wish it was better.”

God promises a perfect eternity for the reborn believer,
with every tear washed away.

If you don’t trust the word of the all-powerful
and all-knowing eternal creator of the universe,
you aren’t thinking —
or you’re thinking far too little
of our infinite God.

On to V7 —

*Thus says the Lord of hosts: Consider your ways.*

This is repetition of V5
where God first told them
to consider their ways.

First God tells them,
consider how you have labored in vain.

Then He tells them,
consider how you can labor in obedience.

Following that, V8 —
God’s call to action:

*Go up to the hills and bring wood and build the house, that I may take pleasure in it and that I may be glorified, says the Lord.*

Go to the hills,
bring the wood,
build the house —
so that I may take pleasure in it
and be glorified.

God connects the temple’s rebuilding
to His own pleasure and glory,
which is interesting,
considering He’s the creator and possessor of all.

He doesn’t need anything from us,
yet He wants our service.

He wants a dwelling amongst His people —
not because He needs shelter,
because He desires proximity
for relationship.

The people needed a house for God
to maintain a symbol of His presence,
to draw their attention to Him,
and to keep the temple traditions
until Jesus arrived to relocate the temple to our spirits.

Yet, until obedience occurs,
covenant discipline continues.

*You looked for much, and behold, it came to little. And when you brought it home, I blew it away. Why? declares the Lord of hosts. Because of my house that lies in ruins, while each of you busies himself with his own house. 10 Therefore the heavens above you have withheld the dew, and the earth has withheld its produce. 11 And I have called for a drought on the land and the hills, on the grain, the new wine, the oil, on what the ground brings forth, on man and beast, and on all their labors.*

In these verses God explains the peoples’ recent frustration:
“You looked for much, and behold, it came to little…
because of my house that lies in ruin.”

Their labor is cursed because their priorities are inverted.
God withheld dew, grain, wine, oil, and productivity
because their self-focus inhibited His worship.

This is Deuteronomic covenant discipline —
not spiteful wrath,
but corrective mercy.

Allow me to clarify:
If you examine Deuteronomy 28,
you’ll see it’s the very consequences that God spelled out
when His people inverted their priorities.

V15 —

*But if you will not obey the voice of the Lord your God or be careful to do all his commandments and his statutes that I command you today, then all these curses shall come upon you and overtake you.*

V22 —

*The Lord will strike you with wasting disease and with fever, inflammation and fiery heat, and with drought and with blight and with mildew.*

V30 —

*You shall plant a vineyard, but you shall not enjoy its fruit.*

V33 —

*A nation that you have not known shall eat up the fruit of your ground and of all your labors, and you shall be only oppressed and crushed continually.*

V38-40 —

*You shall carry much seed into the field and shall gather in little, for the locust shall consume it. 39 You shall plant vineyards and dress them, but you shall neither drink of the wine nor gather the grapes, for the worm shall eat them. 40 You shall have olive trees throughout all your territory, but you shall not anoint yourself with the oil, for your olives shall drop off.*

Let’s back up and also read V36.

*The Lord will bring you and your king whom you set over you to a nation that neither you nor your fathers have known. And there you shall serve other gods of wood and stone.*

This is not directly related
to the consequences that we just read
in Haggai chapter 1, verses 9-11,

but I want to point it out
because V36 is fulfilled through Babylon’s exile
of the people from Israel —
the very exile that the remnant from Haggai
had just returned from.

These scriptures were known to Israel
in Haggai’s time.
They had the means
to predict their current situation
and avoid it.

Yet they still had to see the covenant discipline enacted
to understand and obey.

They were victims of their own negligence
to the warning that God delivered through Moses,
while He followed the covenant to the letter.

The same is true of us.
We have the OT AND the NT,
full of all sorts of prophecies and wisdom —
lessons learned the hard way,
and lessons straight from the mouth of God —

yet with each generation
we have to test the wisdom of our fathers.

We have to run with our eyes closed
and split our forehead on a t-post
to believe that it’s not wise.

I can feel my old scar just thinking about it.

### SECTION II — THE PEOPLE OBEY THE LORD

Moving on to the next section.

*Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, with all the remnant of the people, obeyed the voice of the Lord their God, and the words of Haggai the prophet, as the Lord their God had sent him. And the people feared the Lord. 13 Then Haggai, the messenger of the Lord, spoke to the people with the Lord’s message, “I am with you, declares the Lord.” 14 And the Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and the spirit of Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and the spirit of all the remnant of the people. And they came and worked on the house of the Lord of hosts, their God, 15 on the twenty-fourth day of the month, in the sixth month, in the second year of Darius the king.*

Let’s back-up to V12 again.

This verse tells us that
Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the remnant
obeyed the Word of the LORD
and feared the LORD.

This echoes the principle that says
the fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom —
Proverbs 9:10.

Obedience is the purest expression of fearing the LORD.
The proximity of obedience to fear of the LORD
is not an accident in this text.

For those of you who have children,
and love them enough to discipline them,
what does a child’s fear —
their submissive respect for a higher authority —
naturally lead to?

Obedience.

What is God constantly demanding of his people
throughout the entire Bible?
It’s not success.
It’s not perfect answers for every question.

It’s obedience.

As though to emphasize this point,
V13 provides the first divine encouragement
in this book:

*Then Haggai, the messenger of the Lord, spoke to the people with the Lord’s message, “I am with you, declares the Lord.”*

“I am with you, declares the LORD.”

This is covenant presence —
not architectural, geographical, or physical —
It’s relational.

First, they obeyed with respect,
then He showed His approval
through reaffirming His relationship with them
as their covenantal protector and provider.

In V14 –15 God stirs the spirit of:
Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the remnant.

*And the Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and the spirit of Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and the spirit of all the remnant of the people. And they came and worked on the house of the Lord of hosts, their God, 15 on the twenty-fourth day of the month, in the sixth month, in the second year of Darius the king.*

Once again, it’s ALL of the people —
politicians, priests, populace —
No one escapes the call.

That order refers to decreasing scope
of a person’s immediate material responsibilities —
not their intrinsic value,
or their need for relationship with God.

Allow me to digress for a moment.

I don’t use the word “need”
like my toddler does when she needs a cookie.
When I label something a need,
I tend to mean that it’s quite literally a necessity.

You NEED relationship with God.
He is the source of your existence,
and your ONLY hope for eternity.

In case He seems a little too far off
and spiritually immaterial for you,
He sent His begotten son,
Jesus Christ,
to die for you,
then to rise from the grave for you,
and now to carry you into the eternal glory of God.

Do yourself a favor
that will outperform any diet or exercise routine:
Obey the LORD,
Take the open hand of Jesus,
and live to see the greater glory
that we’ll discuss further
in the next section.

Back to the text —
V14-15 tells us,
that the people’s spirits were stirred up,
and they began the work on the 24th day
of the 6th month.

There are 23 days between V1 and V15,
from the call to the beginning of the work.

This demonstrates that:
divine stirring does not replace human initiative,
and human initiative does not replace divine stirring.

Both operate in synergy.

If you think God is too demanding,
listen to this:

God helps them to obey Himself!

All they had to do was submit
in a proper reverential and
respectful fear of the LORD,
and He stirred up their spirits
to facilitate their continued obedience to Him.

I experienced this divine-human synergy personally
when I finally said yes to the call to lead a Bible study.

Now I wake up and fall asleep
thinking about studying and preaching The Word
every — single — day.

Let’s get back to the text now.

### SECTION III — THE COMING GLORY OF THE TEMPLE

I’m going to read and discuss
the first 9 verses of chapter 2
to complete this arc
from humbling to obedience,
followed by encouragement,
before the focus shifts in V10,
which is where part 2 of this study will resume.

*In the seventh month, on the twenty-first day of the month, the word of the Lord came by the hand of Haggai the prophet: 2 “Speak now to Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and to all the remnant of the people, and say, 3 ‘Who is left among you who saw this house in its former glory? How do you see it now? Is it not as nothing in your eyes? 4 Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel, declares the Lord. Be strong, O Joshua, son of Jehozadak, the high priest. Be strong, all you people of the land, declares the Lord. Work, for I am with you, declares the Lord of hosts, 5 according to the covenant that I made with you when you came out of Egypt. My Spirit remains in your midst. Fear not. 6 For thus says the Lord of hosts: Yet once more, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth and the sea and the dry land. 7 And I will shake all nations, so that the treasures of all nations shall come in, and I will fill this house with glory, says the Lord of hosts. 8 The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, declares the Lord of hosts. 9 The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former, says the Lord of hosts. And in this place I will give peace, declares the Lord of hosts.’”*

*[Aside regarding prosperity preaching]*

Now, back-up to V1 of chapter 2.

By the 21st day of the 7th month,
another message comes.

This is the last day
of the Feast of Tabernacles,
or the Feast of Booths —
a time of rejoicing,
yet also a time of remembrance of dependence.

This feast commemorates Israel’s time in the wilderness,
when they lived in temporary shelters, booths,
and depended entirely on God for provision.

It is held from the 15th through the 21st day of the 7th month,
which means Haggai’s second message
is delivered on the final day of this feast.

This is significant:

The people were meant to be rejoicing
in God’s past faithfulness,
yet they stood discouraged by present hardship
and the unfinished temple.

Into that contrast,
God speaks a promise
of His ongoing presence
and future glory.

Now, the message delivered
by the hand of Haggai:

*“Speak now to Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and to all the remnant of the people, and say, 3 ‘Who is left among you who saw this house in its former glory? How do you see it now? Is it not as nothing in your eyes?*

God acknowledges their melancholic demoralization:
“Who is left among you
who saw this house in its former glory?”

The elders who remembered Solomon’s temple
would have seen the neglected foundation as nothing —
as a shameful ruin.

Their grief would have led to communal dejection —
a state of low-spirited depression amongst the people.

It doesn’t end there, though.
Keep reading.

*Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel, declares the Lord. Be strong, O Joshua, son of Jehozadak, the high priest. Be strong, all you people of the land, declares the Lord. Work, for I am with you, declares the Lord of hosts, 5 according to the covenant that I made with you when you came out of Egypt. My Spirit remains in your midst. Fear not.*

In V4 –5 God says,
“Be strong… for I am with you.”
“My Spirit remains in your midst.”

Divine presence is the sustaining power
for strength,
for obedience,
for perseverance.

Fear not,
for I am here.

That was His message to them.
That is His message to you.

Returning to V6 and 7 —

*For thus says the Lord of hosts: Yet once more, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth and the sea and the dry land. 7 And I will shake all nations, so that the treasures of all nations shall come in, and I will fill this house with glory, says the Lord of hosts.*

God will “shake the nations,”
and “the treasures of all nations shall come in.”

This refers first to the immediate future —
Darius’s treasury funding the temple,
for example,
which is mentioned in Ezra 6 —
but also to the long-term prophetic horizon:
Isaiah 60, and Revelation 21.

Let’s examine those passages briefly,
beginning with Isaiah 60 V1.

*Arise, shine, for your light has come, and the glory of the Lord has risen upon you. 2 For behold, darkness shall cover the earth, and thick darkness the peoples; but the Lord will arise upon you, and his glory will be seen upon you. 3 And nations shall come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your rising.*

This passage talks about glorious light,
which pairs with Haggai’s prophecy of future glory
from V9 of chapter 2.

V5,6, and 11 —

*Then you shall see and be radiant; your heart shall thrill and exult, because the abundance of the sea shall be turned to you, the wealth of the nations shall come to you. 6 A multitude of camels shall cover you, the young camels of Midian and Ephah; all those from Sheba shall come. They shall bring gold and frankincense, and shall bring good news, the praises of the Lord.*

*Your gates shall be open continually; day and night they shall not be shut, that people may bring to you the wealth of the nations, with their kings led in procession.*

The wealth of nations,
the good news,
and the praises of the LORD
shall come to you
with their kings led in procession.

V17 —

*Instead of bronze I will bring gold, and instead of iron I will bring silver; instead of wood, bronze, instead of stones, iron. I will make your overseers peace and your taskmasters righteousness.*

I will give you peace,
and peace will rule over you.

These clearly echo the prophecy
from Haggai chapter 2 V6-9.

Let’s now examine Revelation chapter 21
for its connections to Haggai chapter 2.

Revelation uses the same imagery
of shaking the heavens,
the earth,
and the nations,
which is fulfilled in the new creation.

V1-4 —

*Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea was no more. 2 And I saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. 3 And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, “Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God. 4 He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away.”*

This is the final shaking
of the heavens and the earth.

Even the sea, also mentioned in Haggai 2:6,
is addressed in Revelation,
“and the sea was no more.”

The shalom —
the peace in Haggai
finds its final fulfillment
in the perfect peace of the new creation.

The new Jerusalem —
the city of peace,
composed of “His people” —
descends from Heaven to the new creation —
the church, adorned as Christ’s bride —
to dwell in the house of the LORD,
which is now all of the reborn creation,
without the pain and sorrow of this life.

V22 says —

*And I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb.*

Haggai 2:7 says that God will
fill His house with glory.

The glory of Revelation 21:1-4
combined with V22
ultimately fulfills this prophecy.

There will be no further need for lumber and stone
for the house of the LORD.

We will never not be in the temple —
the house and presence of God —
in the new creation.

V24-26 say —

*By its light will the nations walk, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it, 25 and its gates will never be shut by day—and there will be no night there. 26 They will bring into it the glory and the honor of the nations.*

Once again,
the nations and kings of the earth
will bring their glory to the house of God.

There will be no night, no darkness,
because God’s radiant presence will be our son.

It’s amazing to consider how God thinks;
how perfectly He planned
these histories and prophecies
with clear double-horizons
in the near and distant futures.

Persia sends funds to Israel to restore the temple BC.
The whole world brings glory to the temple in the new creation.

Returning to Haggai 2:8 —

*The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, declares the Lord of hosts.*

This is a reminder:
God does not need their wealth.
God owns all wealth.
He is the creator and rightful possessor
of all things.

V9 —

*“The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former, says the Lord of hosts. And in this place I will give peace, declares the Lord of hosts.”*

As we’ve already established,
particularly in discussing Isaiah and Revelation,
the immediate fulfillment occurs
when Christ enters the temple during His earthly ministry —

but the ultimate fulfillment occurs
through the eschatological dwelling of God with His people —
for the rest of eternity.

“And in this place, God will give you peace.”

### REFLECTION

In this passage, Haggai confronts us
with a truth that never ages:

You can work tirelessly,
plan meticulously,
invest fully —
yet still lack the blessing of God
if you have neglected His instruction.

But the moment we realign ourselves with His priorities,
the moment obedience rises out of holy reverence,
God speaks these words:

“Fear not. I am with you.”

And that presence changes everything.

Because the glory of God’s presence
is greater than the glory of any house we build,
and the peace He gives is better than the success we seek.
