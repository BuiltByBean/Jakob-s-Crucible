### I. SCRIPTURE

The first statement that I want to make
is in regards to Scripture —
the very Word of God.

The Holy Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments,
which comprise the Bible,
are the written Word of God.

All Scripture is authored by God
via the inspiration of the Holy Spirit
expressed through the hands of the human writers.

It carries the authority of God
over all tradition, philosophy, and cultural ideology.

Scripture is inerrant
and therefore a trustworthy source of truth
sufficient for all doctrine, correction,
and instruction in righteousness.

The Scriptural sources that I chose to point to
in support of this statement are
Psalm 19:7–11, Mark 7:1–13, Colossians 2:8,
2 Timothy 3:16–17, and 2 Peter 1:20–21.

and briefly state why I chose to reference them.

a detailed defense of interpretation in this episode
since the purpose of this message
is to confess and reflect on the critical elements

The second statement that I’m going to give
is a summary of the known
critical characteristics of God
according to his revelation.

No man knows everything
that there is to know about God,
so don’t allow the following
to flatten your view of God
to this simple list of facts.

That said,
we can say with certainty
that the God of the Bible is the one true God.
He is the eternal non-created sovereign —
the creator and sustainer of all things.
He is holy, just, merciful, loving,
omniscient, omnipotent, and omnipresent.
According to his nature,
He is unchanging in essence, character, and purposes.
Furthermore, He desires to be known by his creation.

My chosen references for this statement are
Genesis 1:1; Deuteronomy 32:4; Nehemiah 9:6;
Psalm 90:2, 99:9, and 139; Isaiah 45:5–7; Malachi 3:6;
1 Corinthians 8:4–6; Colossians 1:16–17;

I’ll next read those passages
to show how they clearly reveal the truth
of the monotheistic God of all creation.

Psalm 90:2

### III. THE TRINITY – ONE BEING, THREE PERSONS

Statement #3 is about the trinitarian existence of God. After reading the references for this statement I’ll proceed with nested statements regarding each of the three persons of the Trinity.

God exists eternally as one divine being expressed through three distinct persons — each fully God, sharing the same essence, character, and purposes. The three persons of the Trinity revealed through Scripture are Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

My chosen references are Matthew 28:19 and 2 Corinthians 13:14.

I’ll now read each of these passages from the Word of God and reveal in brief why I chose to cite them.

Matthew 28:19

Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

Baptism and discipleship are for Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Since baptism is an expression of faith leading to discipleship for the one true God revealed throughout the Bible, this indicates that all three persons — Father, Son, and Spirit — are God. Yet if He were only one at a time then we would not need to baptize in the singular name of Father, Son, “AND” Holy Spirit — which is the one being, Yahweh. We would only need to baptize in one of God’s names (plural) if He was one person switching modes in a multi-personal dance reminiscent of stage theater or dissociative identity disorder.

2 Corinthians 13:14

The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all.

This Scripture is special not in the identification of characteristics unique to one person or another within the Trinity, but in the revelation of God’s traits with a unified will demonstrated through three persons each fulfilling a distinct role despite their equal nature. The Trinity is full of grace because of love which is expressed through relationship. This is taught to us primarily through the Holy Spirit by the example of Christ according to the Father’s authoritative initiation which enables us to have a relationship with Him.

### A. God the Father

The Father initiates creation and redemption. He is not before the other persons of the Trinity, but He is the one to whom they willingly submit to relationally. His authority as Father is not established through domination, but through the unified will of God.

My chosen references are John 6:44-46 and Ephesians 1:3–11.

I’ll now read each passage and point out the reason for its inclusion in this list.

John 6:44-46

No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him. And I will raise him up on the last day. **45** It is written in the Prophets, ‘And they will all be taught by God.’ Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to Me — **46** not that anyone has seen the Father except He who is from God; He has seen the Father.

This Scripture demonstrates that it is the initiation of God the Father to draw people towards Himself that leads people to God the Son. It also reveals that He who was sent by God, which is Jesus referring to Himself, is the only one who has seen the Father. It also implies that despite Jesus Christ also being God He submitted to the initiation of the Father to be sent into creation.

Ephesians 1:3–11

Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places, **4** even as He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before Him. In love **5** He predestined us for adoption to Himself as sons through Jesus Christ, according to the purpose of his will, **6** to the praise of his glorious grace, with which He has blessed us in the Beloved. **7** In Him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of His grace, **8** which He lavished upon us, in all wisdom and insight **9** making known to us the mystery of his will, according to his purpose, which He set forth in Christ **10** as a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in Him, things in heaven and things on earth. **11** In Him we have obtained an inheritance, having been predestined according to the purpose of Him who works all things according to the counsel of his will.

This passage of Scripture tells us that God the Father blessed us through Christ — God the Son — according to His love and foreknowledge of us from before we were made.

### B. God the Son – Jesus Christ

Jesus Christ is the only person begotten of the Father. He is eternally fully God and through the incarnation also fully man. He lived a sinless life as a man to pay the price of humanity’s sins through crucifixion. He resurrected bodily from the grave and ascended to Heaven by His own righteousness to intercede on our behalf and pave the way for our salvation. Similar to His miraculous ascension into heaven on a cloud, He will return to Earth in the future to bring judgement against evil and bring all of His people into the presence of God. This is the good news of Jesus Christ — the Gospel.

My chosen references are Isaiah 53; John 1:1–14 and 3:13; Acts 1:9–11; 1 Corinthians 15:3-8; Philippians 2:5-11; Colossians 2:9; and Hebrews 1:1-13, 2:14-18, and 4:15.

Next, I’ll read those excerpts from the Bible and explain why they made the list.

Isaiah 53

And to whom has The Arm of the Lord been revealed? **2** For He grew up before Him like a young plant, and like a root out of dry ground; He had no form or majesty that we should look at Him, and no beauty that we should desire Him. **3** He was despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief; and as one from whom men hide their faces He was despised, and we esteemed Him not. **4** Surely He has borne our griefs and carried our sorrows; yet we esteemed Him stricken, smitten by God, and afflicted. **5** But He was pierced for our transgressions; He was crushed for our iniquities; upon Him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed.

Either way, at least half a millennium prior to the live events of the Gospel, the book of Isaiah had already described The Arm of the God incarnated into man without visible sign of divinity, despised and rejected by His community, lacking sin and yet bearing the sins of the people who hated Him as He was crushed to the point of death as an offering so that He could intercede on the peoples’ behalf with God.

This is one of the most incredible and obvious instances of OT prophecy fulfilled in the NT.

John also tells us here that Jesus came as a man, unknown to His people, the people of Israel, to give the right of adoption into God’s family.

John 3:13

In saying that Jesus ascended it is revealed that He entered the presence of God by His own authority. He was not taken up by God as had been done to Enoch and Elijah. He ascended into heaven because He was the one who came from heaven to begin with.

Acts 1:9–11

This part of Scripture summarizes the nature of the incarnation of God to make clear that God the Son humbled Himself to be incarnated into flesh — fully man and yet also fully God.

Colossians 2:9

For in Him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily.

If you had any doubt that Philippians 2 preserved the full deity of Christ while in the incarnation, this verse from Colossians 2 should put that doubt to rest. The whole fullness of deity dwelt in the body of Christ. It was not a mixture, trade, or confusion of natures. It was two fully complete natures present in the incarnation.

Hebrews 1:1-13

If all that still isn’t enough for you to affirm the deity of Christ, God refers to Christ as God explicitly when the Father said to Jesus, “Your throne, O God, is forever and ever”.

Hebrews 2:14-18

This is the Scripture that explains the need for God to be incarnated as man instead of coming in some other divine or angelic form. To establish the kind of relationship that He sought with his human creations it was necessary for Him to take on the weakness of our flesh and let us see Him live as man and give Himself up to death in our place.

This is immensely more meaningful and evidential for God’s love than simply divinely waving away the consequences of sin and death — that He chose to suffer on our behalf and show us how to be holy.

Hebrews 4:15

For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin.

This statement clarifies that even as a man tempted as any other Jesus Christ lived a perfectly sinless life to both sympathize with our difficulties and yet demonstrate the standard of God’s righteousness.

### C. God the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is also fully God, proceeding from the Father through the Son to provide every believer a direct personal relationship with God. The Spirit indwells and empowers, convicts and comforts, sanctifies and regenerates. The gifts of the Holy Spirit are ongoing, and in accordance with the Word must be tested by Scripture to ensure authenticity.

My chosen references are John 14:16–17 and 16:7–15; Acts 5:3–4; Romans 8:11; 1 Corinthians 12–13; Galatians 5:22–25; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–21; Titus 3:4-7; and 1 John 4:1-3.

### IV. HUMANITY, CREATION, & SIN

Statement 4 pertains to the topics of humanity, creation, and sin.

Humanity is created by God, for God, in the image of God, and given life through His breath. Because of these truths, all human life at all stages of life, which begins at conception, belong to God and are worth defending.

Humanity fell under the curse of sin and death through disobedience to God. This curse affects all humans since the first Adam.

My chosen references are Genesis 1:26–27, 2:7, and chapter 3; Psalm 8:4–5 and 139:13-16; Jeremiah 1:5; Luke 1:41; Romans 3:23 and 5:12; and Ephesians 2:1–3.

I didn’t mention this in regards to the persons of the Trinity, but in light of the inspired harmony of all Scripture the phrase “let US make man in OUR image” is the first revelation of the multi-personhood of God from the very first book of the Bible because the words “us” and “our” imply a plurality of persons acting to create in their own image.

For the topic at hand though, this part of the creation story reveals that God created mankind in His image as both male and female. Furthermore, He created mankind to have dominion over the earth and all of its other inhabitants.

### V. SALVATION

Statement 5 is in regards to salvation from the curse of sin and death.

All topics addressed by the Word of God are important, but this is one of the most important subjects for us to be on the same page about and to effectively communicate to the world.

We can tolerate disagreement on secondary issues, but this is THE doctrine impacting the eternal destiny of each human being. So, listen closely, think carefully, and spend more time on this if you are at all unsure of your stance on this issue — or if you are sure but in disagreement with the mainline Christian beliefs that have been established since the days of the early Church.

Here is the statement:

Because God is just and all of mankind have fallen short of the standard of righteousness, we are dependent on God for salvation. Yet God does not force salvation upon us, but by his will He requires that we respond to repent and believe. He judges each person perfectly according to the degree of revelation received. The spiritual rebirth produces good works, but no work of ours can wash clean the stain of our sin. Thus, salvation is acquired only by God’s grace through faith in Christ’s death and resurrection by which He suffered the consequences of sin for all believers despite having never been guilty of sin Himself. Furthermore, even our faith is a gift from God, and yet He still commands us to receive it. By this, God’s gift is shown to be not inconsiderately enforced but lovingly offered.

My chosen references are Joshua 24:15; Mark 1:15; Luke 12:47–48; John 14:6; Acts 2:38 and 17:30-31; Romans 1:18-21, 2:12-16, and 3:19-31; and Ephesians 2:8–10.

Listen to the clarity that the Word of God provides on this subject as I read through each of those passages and briefly explain their purpose in that list.

This verse affirms the free will of choosing what master we will serve.

Jesus went into Galilee, proclaiming the Gospel of God, and saying “the time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the Gospel.”

This is Christ’s simple command: repent and believe in the Gospel — meaning turn away from your sins and have faith in the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ for salvation.

John 14:6

Jesus says, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.”

Jesus is the way. There is no other way, no side door into heaven, no cheat code to eternity. You are either saved through Jesus or you are destined for Hell.

We are simply called to be obedient in our reception of it and let that faith produce the work of God in us.

### VI. THE CHRISTIAN CHURCH

Statement 6 is a summary explanation of the Church.

The church is the body of Christ, and Christ is the head of the Church. With the tearing of the veil Christ metaphorically proclaimed what was in a later Scripture clearly established: the priesthood of all believers. This was done so that by His intercession with the Father every believer has a direct connection to God through the Holy Spirit. The Great Commission that Christ gave for all members of his Church is to make disciples of all the people of the Earth, baptizing them in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

My chosen references are Matthew 28:18-20; 1 Peter 2:5-9; 1 Corinthians 12:12–27; Ephesians 1:22–23 and 5:23; and Revelation 1:5-6.

Consider how these passages individually and all together inform us of God’s design for the Church.

Matthew 28:18-20

And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.”

This is a depiction of Jesus acting on his authority as the head of the Church by issuing the command to go out and disciple all the people of the world.

### VII. ORDINANCES

Statement 7 is in regards to biblical ordinances.

There are two ordinances, which are visible proclamations of The Faith ordained by Jesus Christ to be practiced through faithful obedience in remembrance of Him until His second coming. They are water baptism and communion.

- **Water Baptism**

Water baptism is an outward expression of inward faith in the cleansing renewal of the Holy Spirit. The ritual is not salvific, but celebratory proclamation of salvation.

My chosen references are Luke 23:39–43, Romans 6:3–4, and Titus 3:5-7.

Let’s confirm the prior statement by examining those scriptures.

Luke 23:39–43

One of the criminals who were hanged railed at him, saying, “Are you not the Christ? Save yourself and us!” **40** But the other rebuked him, saying, “Do you not fear God, since you are under the same sentence of condemnation? **41** And we indeed justly, for we are receiving the due reward of our deeds; but this man has done nothing wrong.” **42** And he said, “Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom.” **43** And He said to Him, “Truly, I say to you, today you will be with Me in paradise.”

This passage does not relate directly to baptism, but it is an example of salvation without water baptism, thus proving that water baptism is not required for salvation. This is why it is described as an outward expression of faith rather than salvific.

Romans 6:3–4

Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? **4** We were buried therefore with Him by baptism into death, in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life.

This tells us that water baptism represents being buried with Christ in his death and then being raised to new life.

Titus 3:5-7

He saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit, **6** whom He poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior, **7** so that being justified by his grace we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life.

This describes the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which is the baptism that every new believer experiences independent of water baptism. Water cannot wash our sins away and justify us before God. Only the cleansing and renewal of the Holy Spirit does that because of the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ.

### B. Communion

Communion is the practice of breaking bread and drinking from the cup with reverent remembrance of our Savior’s sacrifice. The bread represents Christ’s body offered to be broken for us, and the drink of the cup represents Christ’s blood freely poured out for us. We share in communion to remember his sacrifice with the hope of sharing also in his resurrection. The communion table belongs to God and is open to all reborn believers.

My chosen references are Luke 22:19-20 and 1 Corinthians 11:26.

Listen as I read those passages and point out their contribution to the statement on Communion.

Luke 22:19-20

He took bread, and when He had given thanks, He broke it and gave it to them, saying, “This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of Me.” **20** And likewise the cup after they had eaten, saying, “This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.

The scene is set as the Lord’s table as the elements and their purposes are described. In this moment Jesus initiated a tradition of celebrating the new covenant.

1 Corinthians 11:26

As often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord’s death until He comes.

This verse describes communion as a proclamation of faith in Christ which we should practice until his return.

### VIII. CHRISTIAN LIFE & IDENTITY

Statement 8 is in regards to the Christian life and identity.

Through the baptism of the Holy Spirit every believer is baptized into the death of Christ for freedom from death and into the resurrection of Christ for eternal life. Because of this a believer’s life is replaced and sustained by the life of God the Son such that our identity is found in Jesus Christ, our Savior.

However, until the second coming we must continue to endure the fallen creation and wrestle against our sin nature. This requires that we daily die to ourselves and choose to live as Christ so that when we sin, we quickly repent, turning away from the sins of the dead flesh and reminding ourselves of our identity in Christ.

God showed His love for us by sacrificing His Son on the cross while we were still sinners. Therefore, to be Christ-like requires us also to love sinners by rejecting sin and pointing them to the cross where they too can find their identity in Christ.

My chosen references are John 8:10–11; Romans 5:8 and 6:3-10; 1 Corinthians 6:9–11; Galatians 2:20; and Colossians 3:1–11.

Allow me to read the truths of the prior statement directly from the Word of God now.

John 8:10–11

Jesus stood up and said to her, “Woman, where are they? Has no one condemned you?” **11** She said, “No one, Lord.” And Jesus said, “Neither do I condemn you; go, and from now on sin no more.”

God does not forgive our past without addressing our future. The judgement of life with no condemnation comes with the command to sin no more.

Romans 5:8

God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

Simple, straightforward, and incredible is the fact the midst of our rebellion is the scene for God’s greatest expression of love through sacrifice.

The old self of the flesh is a slave chained by sin to death and only freed from sin through the consequence of death, but Jesus has broken the chains of sin that bound us to our cursed fate.

This is how we are saved through Christ: that our identity is no longer in ourselves but in Him as through faith we spiritually share in his death and resurrection and find our identity as written in the book of life no longer in our own insufficient selves but in the perfectly fulfilling identity of our Savior.

### IX. MARRIAGE & SEXUAL ETHICS

Statement number 9 pertain to marriage and sexual ethics.

Marriage is not an invention of humanity, but a discovered gift of God accounted for by his designs for male and female. God defines marriage as one man and one woman, and commands that sexual intimacy is reserved for the private context of marriage not for shame, but for honor.

My chosen references are Genesis 2:24, Song of Solomon 4:16-5:1, Matthew 19:4–9, 1 Corinthians 6:18–20, and Hebrews 13:4.

This has become a rather controversial topic in our culture, despite the relative unity of the Church throughout history on this subject, so the Church needs to be reminded of the clarity of Scripture on this topic.

Genesis 2:24

God describes marriage as none other than a man and a woman leaving their families of origin to be joined intimately in the formation of a new family.

There are descriptions elsewhere in the Bible of other kinds of relationships involving homosexuality and more than two individuals, but this is the prescription for marriage — the one approved model for us to replicate.

This verse beautifully contradicts any negative attitude regarding physical intimacy categorically by explicitly telling us that God sees honor in marriage.

### X. CHURCH LEADERSHIP

Statement 10 regards church leadership.

Church leadership is prescribed by the Word, which reveals Jesus Christ as the head of the Church extending authority through qualified men in the office of biblical eldership. This is not due to the inability of women to lead men, but rather due to the authority of God’s Word.

God the Son submitted humbly to the authority of God the Father through their unified will, without dominance or inequality. Similarly, all members of the Church, both male and female, are to submit humbly to the authority of the Elders as the Elders submit to the authority of Christ. This reflects God’s design for order, responsibility, and love as also evident in both the Trinity and family structures.

To qualify for eldership, a man must have a good reputation according to the expectations of Scripture. He must be sober-minded, disciplined, respectable, hospitable, holy, not arrogant, not a drunkard, not a lover of money, not quarrelsome, and not prone to violence. He must hold firm to the Word of God and be able to teach it and correct those who contradict it. He must manage his household well so that if he has children they are submissive according to The Faith, and if married he must be a faithful man of one wife. He also must not be a recent convert, so that he will be spiritually mature for leadership.

The elders must also be held accountable as unlike Christ they are fallible and not above rebuke.

The references for this section are especially obvious. They are Galatians 2:11–14; 1 Timothy 3:1–8 and 5:19-22; and Titus 1:5–2:1.

While this is not a primary issue for Christian faith, it is still critically important to follow the biblical mandates on this issue, and it’s been a hot topic for quite a long time.

Furthermore, there are an unfortunate quantity of church bodies that are suffering greatly at the hands of unqualified leadership. For this reason, in addition to the simple need to submit to Scripture in all regards, the Church as a whole desperately needs to regain a greater adherence to the scriptural standard for leadership.

Pay attention to the prescription of God’s Word on this topic.

Galatians 2:11–14

We should not wait for outsiders to point out our hypocrisy. We should name it and excise it ourselves, bringing correction wherever possible.

1 Timothy 3:1–8

To the thirsty I will give from the spring of the water of life without payment. **7** The one who conquers will have this heritage, and I will be his God and he will be my son. **8** But as for the cowardly, the faithless, the detestable, as for murderers, the sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars, their portion will be in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur, which is the second death.”

### The New Jerusalem

**9** Then came one of the seven angels who had the seven bowls full of the seven last plagues and spoke to me, saying, “Come, I will show you the Bride, the wife of the Lamb.” **10** And he carried me away in the Spirit to a great, high mountain, and showed me the holy city Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God, **11** having the glory of God, its radiance like a most rare jewel, like a jasper, clear as crystal. **12** It had a great, high wall, with twelve gates, and at the gates twelve angels, and on the gates the names of the twelve tribes of the sons of Israel were inscribed— **13** on the east three gates, on the north three gates, on the south three gates, and on the west three gates. **14** And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and on them were the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb.

**15** And the one who spoke with me had a measuring rod of gold to measure the city and its gates and walls. **16** The city lies foursquare, its length the same as its width. And he measured the city with his rod, 12,000 stadia. Its length and width and height are equal. **17** He also measured its wall, 144 cubits by human measurement, which is also an angel’s measurement. **18** The wall was built of jasper, while the city was pure gold, like clear glass. **19** The foundations of the wall of the city were adorned with every kind of jewel. The first was jasper, the second sapphire, the third agate, the fourth emerald, **20** the fifth onyx, the sixth carnelian, the seventh chrysolite, the eighth beryl, the ninth topaz, the tenth chrysoprase, the eleventh jacinth, the twelfth amethyst. **21** And the twelve gates were twelve pearls, each of the gates made of a single pearl, and the street of the city was pure gold, like transparent glass.

**22** And I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb. **23** And the city has no need of sun or moon to shine on it, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb. **24** By its light will the nations walk, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it, **25** and its gates will never be shut by day—and there will be no night there. **26** They will bring into it the glory and the honor of the nations. **27** But nothing unclean will ever enter it, nor anyone who does what is detestable or false, but only those who are written in the Lamb’s book of life.

For the final judgement, God will raise the dead to be judged. Those not found in Christ will be cast into the lake of fire which is described as an eternal torment, which does not indicate literal annihilation.

After this judgement, those who remain faithful to God will witness the re-perfection of creation through the new creation in which God replaces the sun in the sky with the brilliance of his immediate presence and wipes away every stain resulting from sin — all pain, sorrow, and regret.

Never again after that time will anything unholy be found in all of creation.

If we are consistent with Scripture regarding at least these truths, then we will have a firm foundation for Christian living.
